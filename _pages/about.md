I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://illinois.edu/), advised by [Prof. Gang Wang](https://gangw.cs.illinois.edu/) in the [Siebel School of Computing and Data Science](https://siebelschool.illinois.edu/). My research is in network security, with a focus on abuse and fraud detection. I study how defenders detect and respond to attackers who hide behind shared and disposable IP addresses, such as VPNs, proxies, and cloud servers.

Before UIUC, I received M.S. degrees from the University of Florida and the University of Washington, and a B.A.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from the University of British Columbia.

<summary style="cursor: pointer;"><strong>Prior Research Experience</strong></summary>
<p>At the University of Florida, I worked on security projects involving cellular network security, systems security, and software security. These projects resulted in three published papers, two of them at <a href="https://sp2023.ieee-security.org/">IEEE S&amp;P</a> and <a href="https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23">USENIX Security</a>, and a U.S. patent. At the University of Washington, my studies focused on electrical engineering and machine learning, and I published a reproducibility study in NLP that was presented at <a href="https://nips.cc/Conferences/2022">NeurIPS 2022</a>. I was also a research intern at the University of Washington's <a href="https://www.healthdata.org/">Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME)</a>, where I worked on Global Burden of Disease (GBD) projects on bullying, gun violence, and causes of death and hospitalization. I am a co-author on GBD studies published in <a href="https://www.thelancet.com/">The Lancet</a>.</p>
